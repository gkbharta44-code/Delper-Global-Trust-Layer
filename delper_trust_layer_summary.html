Delper Global Ecommerce Trust Layer Deployment

A self-funded B2B trade rail targeting 70% of a $1.4 trillion high-margin market within five years, built on SAP BTP, Proxtera and milestone-based escrow.

Prepared under a PwC Managed Services framework, Singapore · Delper Pte. Ltd. · 12 October 2026

Executive summary

Delper is positioned as a digital trust utility. Its platform connects an enterprise SAP S/4HANA core to Proxtera's single-endpoint API hub, then automates the trade lifecycle through milestone-based escrow release (Proxtera Protect) and third-party quality control inspection.

The model is self-funding. A $1.2 million seed round (40% equity, 60% venture debt) starts the platform, and all subsequent net cash flow is retained in a ring-fenced reserve that supplies the escrow float. The document projects that reserve reaching $5.12 billion in Year 3 and $341.69 billion in Year 5.

Market and monetization

The platform avoids low-margin commodity trade such as oil, steel and grain. It targets the $2 trillion cross-border high-margin B2B pool: medical devices, pharmaceuticals, wholesale fashion and clean beauty CPG, where wholesale gross margins sit between 35% and 60%.

The 24.2% take rate is split into a 2% sales commission and a 22.2% programmatic lead-generation fee. The document presents this as a replacement for sourcing brokers (5–7%), customs brokerage (3–5%), overseas trade show marketing (10–15%) and letter-of-credit fees (2–4%). An automated re-engagement workflow targets a 50% recovery rate on abandoned carts.

How a transaction clears

Escrow is sized to a rolling 14-day window covering shipping and inspection gates, not to total annual GMV. Fees are expected to outrun that lock-up.

Cross-border buyer locks purchase capital with Tazapay (escrow agent)
Funds held in syndicated vault nodes at Standard Chartered, DBS and HSBC
Released on delivery after a 14-day window and independent inspection

If an SGS inspection fails, a qc_inspection_failed webhook reaches SAP BTP. Escrow is frozen, buyer refund starts, the rejected lot is moved to blocked stock, and the ledger is rebalanced automatically.

Capital structure

Year 1 CapEx of $864K goes to configuring SAP BTP middleware, mapping processes in SAP Signavio and structuring transfer pricing. Reusable API configuration then drops annual CapEx to a $30K maintenance baseline by Year 5. OpEx covers cloud infrastructure, data routing and PwC managed services for compliance, treasury and multi-border VAT/GST processing.

The document states that Year 1 net profit of $3.52 million clears the $720K venture debt principal within the first fiscal cycle.

Risk, controls and regulation

Technical

The architecture routes everything through SAP BTP over a clean S/4HANA core, with a single connection to Proxtera rather than separate integrations for India's ONDC, Shopify Plus and SAP Ariba. Data moves over HTTPS REST or GraphQL, secured with OAuth 2.0 client credentials and TLS 1.3.

Data and privacy

The memorandum says front-end transaction nodes are decoupled from the core database to comply with Singapore's PDPA. Escrow is held by a syndicated tier-1 bank triad, which the document says is structured to satisfy Basel III capital adequacy guidance.

Competition

The document proposes white-labeling the system into separate industry rails (Pharma-Rail, Med-Tech-Rail, Fashion-Rail) so that individual units stay below antitrust trigger levels for the US FTC and European Commission. This is the most sensitive part of the memo and warrants independent legal review before any reliance on it.

Board actions requested

  • Execute the PwC BPO agreement. Onboard the PwC Singapore Trade Advisory Hub, deploy the SAP Signavio process maps and begin filings with the Monetary Authority of Singapore.

The document concludes that the project is approved for immediate implementation, on the basis that the financial model is validated, the architecture is secure and the regulatory perimeter is protected.

Figures check

I recalculated the source figures. Most reconcile, including the fee revenue, cumulative totals, net cash flow and the reserve roll-forward. These points do not:

  • Breakeven GMV units. Dividing total cash outlay by 24.2% gives about $4.14B for Year 3, $39.29B for Year 4 and $220.0B for Year 5. The memo prints $4.14M, $39.29M and $220.03M, which are 1,000 times too small.
  • Typo. OpEx for Year 5 is written as "$28.30,000" in the narrative. The table shows $28.30M.
  • Market share wording. The memo describes a 70% share of a $1.4T market, but elsewhere the $1.4T figure is the Year 5 target inside a $2T pool. The two readings are consistent only if the 70% refers to the pool, which the wording does not make clear.
