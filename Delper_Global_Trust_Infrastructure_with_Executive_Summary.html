3. Revenue Streams

Stream 1: Software take rates

Delper removes a 5.1% share of the traditional friction cost, leaving a take rate of 24.2% on consumer and high-margin goods and 15.184% on premium commodities. Transactions must be at least USD 300.

Stream 2: Liquidity spread

Delper lends from its reserves on 14 to 90 day facilities at a stated 6.5% p.a. Partner banks take a 0.5% p.a. frontending fee, leaving Delper a 6.0% p.a. net spread secured by bonded cargo. On the revenue basis used here, this spread sits inside the 24.2% of turnover, so it is not added on top.

Stream 3: Sovereign BOOT concessions

Delper funds customs ports, logistics hubs, and data centers under 10-year build-own-operate-transfer contracts, and applies the 15.184% rate to port duties and clearing fees. BOOT activity begins in Year 6.

Delper connects global buyers and vendors through one integration layer, holds payment in escrow, checks quality at an independent inspection stage, and sources orders through competitive bidding on price and logistics. Revenue comes from a software take rate, a liquidity spread, and, from Year 6, sovereign BOOT concessions. At 26 turns from Year 2, GMV reaches the 70% cap in Year 11 and is held there. The 70% cap compares transaction volume (GMV), which counts each step of a chain, with GDP, which counts only final output. 10a. Recursive Turnover and End-Customer Test

Capital is not withdrawn. The reinvested capital stays in the ecosystem and funds each 14-day cycle, and only the 40% pool (OpEx, income tax, and CapEx repayment) is paid out. This holds only if vendor payments and buyer collections settle within each cycle, so the full balance is free again at the next turn.

The end customer is the buyer, so turnover is recursive. The same buyer money is counted at each step of a chain. End-customer spend is therefore GMV divided by the chain depth, the number of steps a buyer's dollar passes through before it is consumed. Year 11 GMV of $123.51T is tested below against base world GDP of $176.44T.

  • Settlement within each cycle needs evidence. Any part of the book that settles more slowly cannot turn 26 times.

Reading the scorecard

  • Several KPIs are fixed by assumption. Operating margin, operating cash flow to sales, free cash flow margin, EBITDA to operating cash flow, and the DSCR proxy stay the same every year because the model applies fixed cost ratios. They describe the model's inputs, not performance. Only growth, ROIC, the cash cycle, and working capital move, and they move because of turns and the GDP cap.
  • The Year 11 cap changes the picture. Once GMV is held at 70% of GDP, revenue growth falls to 3.4% in Year 12, ROIC falls from 377% in Year 2 to 176% in Year 11, and working capital rises from 15.9% to 34.0% of revenue.
  • Most of the CapEx repayment stream is unexplained. The stream is 24.15% of revenue, but only the investor's 25% share (6.04%) is described. The other 75%, which is 18.11% of revenue, has no stated recipient. This affects the free cash flow margin and the DSCR proxy, and should be confirmed.
  • Gaps to close before these KPIs can be tested. Gross margin needs direct production costs. The current ratio needs a balance sheet. A true cash conversion cycle needs receivable, payable, and inventory days. Leverage and DSCR need a debt schedule that includes any BOOT project funding.

13. Risks

  • Cap binding: From Year 11, GMV is held at 70% of world GDP while capital keeps growing. Effective turns fall from 26 to 12.1 in Year 11 and 1.6 by Year 15, so the 26-turn assumption stops applying after Year 10.
  • Capacity: 26 turns means a full 14-day cycle every year from Year 2, with no slack for delay, default, or disruption.
  • Year 1 capital: Year 1 turnover of $39.0M rests on $2.214M of trading capital. Set-up spend of $1.786M is not turned over.
  • Demand: Year 11 volume of $123.51T needs a market about 13.5 times the addressable pool the document sizes.
  • Customer pricing: Passing all other costs to customers raises the landed price. That weakens the best-price claim and may reduce volume.
  • Integration dependency: Disruption of SAP BTP Event Mesh or Proxtera endpoints would create transaction backlogs.
  • Regulatory exposure: Antitrust review by the US FTC or European Commission remains a risk. The antitrust immunity claim for BOOT concessions has no cited legal basis.
  • Banking treatment: Basel III ratios apply to a bank's own capital, so the treatment of reserves inside partner banks needs a regulatory opinion.
  • Licensing and settlement: Netting cannot remove settlement with parties outside the network, and licences for payment, lending, and custody in each market need confirming.
  • Income tax basis: Tax is 17% of pretax profit, with pretax taken as 72.3% of revenue. The pretax base needs confirmation with a Singapore tax adviser.

14. Open Items Before Investment

  • Evidence that the plan can sustain 26 turns from Year 2, the full 14-day cycle.
  • Evidence that the market could supply GMV at 70% of world GDP by Year 11, and the basis for the GDP path.
  • Confirmation of the pretax base, including whether CapEx repayment is deductible, and the income tax treatment, with a Singapore tax adviser.
  • Treatment of the $1.0M liquidity reserve as CapEx or working capital, to confirm with an auditor.
  • Signed agreements or public confirmation for Proxtera, Tazapay, SGS, and each partner bank and government partner.
  • Escrow, bidding, logistics, and all other costs passed to customers, which are not yet costed in the investment plan. Confirm the principal or agent treatment with the accountant.
  • Legal basis for antitrust immunity and the regulatory treatment of reserves under Basel III.
  • Average chain depth for the commodity and e-commerce flows, which determines how much GMV becomes final consumption.
  • Evidence that vendor payments and buyer collections settle within each 14-day cycle, with no withdrawal of reinvested capital.
  • Option pool terms, which would reduce the investor's 25% stake.

15. Verdict

The plan is structurally coherent: a $4.0M CapEx programme is funded by the investment, Year 1 operating costs are met from revenue, and the investor is repaid from its share of the CapEx stream. With all reserve funds carried forward, turnover capped at 26, and GMV capped at 70% of world GDP, the plan repays the investor in about 2.0 years and reaches the GMV cap in Year 11. After that year, the cap limits growth to GDP, and turnover falls as capital outgrows it. Holding 70% of world GDP as transaction volume is a strong demand assumption that the document does not evidence. Treat the figures as projections, and confirm the open items before any commitment.

1. Strategic Intent & Vision

Delper is deploying a highly scalable, asset-light B2B supply chain, digital trade orchestration, and marketing management architecture. The operational infrastructure launches with a USD 100 million per annum global pilot for a premium Mexican Tequila brand, using Malaysia as the proof-of-concept country node.

This deployment establishes the definitive blueprint for a 10-year global scale-up. By integrating with Proxtera Singapore, Delper will systematically ingest, market, and manage cross-border supply chains for one billion products currently under-monetised on static e-commerce platforms (such as Shopify and WooCommerce).

2. Core Operational & Financial Architecture

The Delper framework re-engineers traditional trade routes into a friction-free, risk-insulated ecosystem driven by three core pillars:

  • Risk-Insulated Del Credere Mandate: Delper acts as the global Del Credere Agent for the principal. While Delper technically guarantees the financial solvency of the distribution channel, structural payment rules eliminate actual default exposure.
  • The Advance-Payment Guardrail: Local distributors must pay in full, in advance, prior to any inventory being released from regional hubs. This structural requirement drives credit and bad-debt risk down to absolute zero, bypassing the need for heavy working capital reserves or expensive trade credit insurance.
  • Tax-Deferred Bonded Logistics: Bulk inventory is shipped directly into Delper-managed Bonded Warehouses located within the destination countries. Stock is held securely under a duty-deferred structure. Import duties, local excise taxes, and custom clearances are triggered and liquidated only after advance payments clear, creating a highly efficient cash-conversion cycle.

They carry no commercial retail stock; all end-customer and sub-retailer interactions occur via customized QR codes that trigger digital transactions.

B. Distributor Display Windows (Decentralized Media Asset)

  • Mandatory Network Covenants: Every distributor appointed by Delper must dedicate a premium, highly visible, street- or lobby-facing retail window strictly as a Delper Exhibition Asset.
  • Programmatic Shelf-Space Network: As the model scales to ingest one billion products via Proxtera, these windows become programmable physical real estate. The Delper core engine dynamically changes and targets what products are physically exhibited based on localized neighborhood demographic data.
  • Omnichannel Incentivization: Passing foot traffic and sub-retail buyers interact with the display window and scan built-in checkout QR codes. Sales generated through a specific window award a micro-commission to that distributor, ensuring they proactively maintain the aesthetic and premium standards of the display asset.

4. The 10-Year Technology & Aggregation Flywheel

By transitioning from a single highly regulated commodity (Tequila) to a multi-category product network via Proxtera Singapore, Delper addresses the structural fragmentation plaguing global small-to-medium digital merchants.

  • Unified API Ingestion: Native plugins deployed on merchant Shopify and WooCommerce storefronts automatically feed SKU data, Harmonized System (HS) customs codes, and structural metadata into Delper's core middleware.
  • Open Trade Protocols: Delper's middleware normalizes multi-platform product feeds into standardized B2B open-trade JSON schemas, instantly publishing global merchant inventory across Proxtera's cross-border buyer networks.
  • Automated Fulfillment Triggers: Institutional banking APIs are hardwired into the warehouse management systems (WMS). The precise millisecond an advance payment clears escrow, the local bonded warehouse automated systems instantly print picking slips and trigger customs clearance routing.

5. Monetization & Value Proposition

Delper's structural positioning unlocks highly resilient, multi-tiered cash-flow engines:

  • Supply Chain & Agency Margins: Standard logistics orchestration fees and premium Del Credere service commissions paid by the anchor suppliers.
  • Shelf-Space-as-a-Service (SSaaS): Recurring global subscription fees paid by e-commerce merchants to have their digital products physically showcased across both the Flagship Experience Centers and the massive network of Distributor Display Windows.
  • Franchise Fees & Royalties: Upfront territory fees from Master Franchisees combined with digital transactional volume splits from physical real estate traffic attribution.
