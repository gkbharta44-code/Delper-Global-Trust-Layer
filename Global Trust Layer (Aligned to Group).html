Delper Global Trust Infrastructure: Executive Summary and Investment Plan

Revised to the confirmed model: 15.184% of GMV, $0.86M GMV base, $2.60M pre-shipment margin (margin only), 40% OpEx, 17% tax, and 40% to pooled bond debt (not to GK Bharta) with 60% reinvested from Year 2, as set out in the Delper Group Executive Summary. 3. Revenue Streams

Stream 1: Software take rate

Track 1 (bulk trade) charges a 15.184% fixed transaction rate on all GMV. Track 2 (Global Retail) charges 24.2%, which is 24.22% gross profit per $300 order in the group summary. Transactions must be at least USD 300.

Stream 2: Liquidity spread

Delper lends from its reserves on 14 to 90 day facilities at a stated 6.5% p.a. Partner banks take a 0.5% p.a. frontending fee, leaving Delper a 6.0% p.a. net spread secured by bonded cargo. The spread is not added on top of the 15.184% transaction rate.

Stream 3: Sovereign BOOT concessions

Delper funds customs ports, logistics hubs, and data centers under 10-year build-own-operate-transfer contracts, and applies the 15.184% rate to port duties and clearing fees. BOOT activity begins in Year 6. BOOT revenue is not included in the figures in this document, and the group summary does not include it in its figures either.

4. Turnover Targets

Turnover is 17.6 in Year 1 on $0.86M of trading capital, and 26 from Year 2, the full capacity of a 14-day cycle. GMV is opening capital times turns. There is no GMV cap: 60% of after-tax profit is reinvested from Year 2, so capital and GMV grow from that base and stay far below world GDP.

12. Risks

  • Reinvestment dependence: only 60% of after-tax profit is reinvested from Year 2, so growth depends on 26 turns a year and on settlement within each cycle.
  • Capacity: 26 turns means a full 14-day cycle every year from Year 2, with no slack for delay, default, or disruption.
  • Year 1 capital: Year 1 turnover of $15.14M rests on $0.86M of trading capital. The $2.60M margin is revolving and is not turned over in the GMV base.
  • Demand: Year 10 GMV of $11.39B is about 0.12% of the $9.15T addressable pool the document sizes.
  • Customer pricing: passing all other costs to customers raises the landed price. That weakens the best-price claim and may reduce volume.
  • Integration dependency: disruption of SAP BTP Event Mesh or Proxtera endpoints would create transaction backlogs.
  • Group basis: the group summary calculates bulk repayments on 40% of revenue and holds bulk GMV flat from Year 5. This document uses 40% of after-tax profit and grows GMV to Year 10. The two bases must be reconciled before the group ledger is relied on (Section 16).
  • Regulatory exposure: antitrust review by the US FTC or European Commission remains a risk. The antitrust immunity claim for BOOT concessions has no cited legal basis.
  • Banking treatment: Basel III ratios apply to a bank's own capital, so the treatment of reserves inside partner banks needs a regulatory opinion.
  • Licensing and settlement: netting cannot remove settlement with parties outside the network, and licences for payment, lending, and custody in each market need confirming.
  • Income tax basis: tax is 17% of pretax profit, with pretax taken as revenue less OpEx. The pretax base needs confirmation with a Singapore tax adviser.

13. Open Items Before Investment

  • Evidence that the plan can sustain 26 turns from Year 2, the full 14-day cycle.
  • Evidence of market demand for Year 10 GMV, and the basis for the GDP reference path.
  • Confirmation of the pretax base and the income tax treatment, with a Singapore tax adviser.
  • Treatment of the $2.60M pre-shipment margin, and whether the $1.00M reserve and $0.35M contingency sit in the margin only, with an auditor. The group summary treats the full $2.60M as revolving margin, while this document also places $1.25M of local partner, office, technology and Singapore costs inside it. Both cannot be true.
  • Signed agreements or public confirmation for Proxtera, Tazapay, SGS, OCBC Singapore, Axis Bank, and each partner bank and government partner. None is signed as of this document.
  • Escrow, bidding, logistics, and all other costs passed to customers, which are not yet costed in the investment plan. Confirm the principal or agent treatment with the accountant.
  • Legal basis for antitrust immunity and the regulatory treatment of reserves under Basel III.
  • Average chain depth for the commodity and e-commerce flows, which determines how much GMV becomes final consumption.
  • Evidence that vendor payments and buyer collections settle within each 14-day cycle.
  • Palm oil deal: evidence of USD 15.00M a year (1,000,000 metric tons at USD 15 per ton) to fund the Year 1 loan repayment and the Radiant payment.
  • Legal documents for the Track 1 loan and the 40% equity terms.
  • Year 1 funding: confirm that the palm oil facility covers the $8.88M investor loan repayment and the $4.00M Radiant payment, and agree the pro-rata split between Track 1 and Track 2.
  • Escrow bank: the group summary names Axis Bank for escrow in Track 2 and in its milestones. This document names Tazapay for Track 1 escrow. Confirm the escrow provider for each track.
  • Palm oil sourcing: the group requires a certification and deforestation-free sourcing policy (RSPO or equivalent, with EUDR due diligence) before the palm oil route begins.
  • Automated decisions: the group requires a tested human override, a named owner and an audit log for automated customs and trade decisions. Add these to the bidding, release and customs controls.
  • Group ledger: the group summary must re-base its bulk repayments and bulk GMV path to match this model, or this model must adopt the group's basis (Section 16).

14. Verdict

The plan is structurally coherent: a $4.0M investment is deployed as $0.86M of trading capital, $0.54M of spent set-up, and a $2.60M margin. Year 1 operating costs are met from revenue, and 60% of after-tax profit is reinvested from Year 2. Track 1 reaches $1.73B of revenue in Year 10 on the confirmed terms. Its outcome depends on sustaining 26 turns and on market demand for the volume required. Treat the figures as projections, and confirm the open items before any commitment. Track 1 is aligned with the group summary on fee, turns, spread, loan and equity terms. The bulk repayment basis and GMV path in the group ledger still need re-basing (Section 16).

15. Exit Paths

The Track 1 investor loan is repaid in Year 1 from palm oil revenue, with Track 1 after-tax profit contributing. At Month 12, the investor's 40% equity stake and Radiant Synage's 60% stake pass to GK Bharta, as the group summary sets out. No external exit route is needed.
