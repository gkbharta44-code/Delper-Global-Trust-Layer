1. What Track A does

Track A is the bulk enterprise trade line of the Delper Global Trust Infrastructure. Customers pay in advance by cash or letter of credit, so no customer receivable is carried. Delper charges a fixed 15.184% fee on gross merchandise value (GMV) and earns a 6.0% net annual spread on asset-secured facilities that use bonded cargo as collateral. A $2.60M pre-shipment escrow margin bridges supplier and freight payments and is recycled as payments come in.

Market reference and bulk rate. Track A charges a fixed 15.184% of GMV. Its $3.15T fee from Year 7 is about 2.5% of 79% of world GDP in Year 7, and about 1.1% in Year 28, measured against world GDP per user of 79% of GDP divided by 6.0 billion users ($19,585 in Year 5 and $21,183 in Year 7). The 79% in the Track A volume target refers to world merchandise exports on a $26.26T base, not to GDP.

Not included in group figures for Track A and excluded from this document. Any BOOT revenue from Year 6 would be additional to the figures below.

7. Investor loan and Year 1 repayment

The investor's $8.00M principal carries 11% interest and is repaid in Year 1 as $8.88M, out of palm oil revenue of $15.00M a year (coverage floor $12.88M). The same palm oil revenue also pays the $4.00M Radiant Synage payment. Track A carries $4.00M of the principal, and its pro-rata interest share is $0.44M, giving $4.44M. That split is confirmed as correct. Track A earns no fee in Year 1 in the group ledger, so the Year 1 repayment is funded entirely by palm oil revenue.

At Month 12 the investor's 40% stake and Radiant Synage's 60% stake pass to GK Bharta, which becomes the 100% owner from Year 2.

8. Debt and distributions

Track A fee income is pooled with the other revenue streams. The pool pays 17% tax, then the bonds. The bonds total $102.00T: $46.00T of Iron Nexus and $56.00T of Bharat. Iron Nexus is retired at the end of Year 18. Bharat is issued at $53.36T at the end of Year 18, carries 9% interest from Year 20, and is cleared in Year 28. Distributions to GK Bharta start in Year 28.

The 40% pooled and 60% reinvested rule applies to Track B (Retail) only, as the group summary sets it out. The earlier draft applied it to Track A as well; that has been removed.

9. Risks

  • Volume. The group treats bulk volume as a stress case, and the volume itself is not sourced in the group summary. Reaching the group's ledger volume depends on demand that neither document evidences.
  • Standalone capacity. The bottom-up test is far below the volume the group's ledger implies. Growth in the standalone plan depends on 26 turns a year and on settlement within each 14-day cycle.
  • Settlement and licensing. Netting cannot remove settlement with parties outside the network. Payment, lending and custody licences are needed in each market.
  • Antitrust. Review by the US FTC or European Commission remains a risk. No legal basis is cited for any antitrust immunity claim.
  • Banking treatment. Basel III ratios apply to a bank's own capital, so the treatment of reserves inside partner banks needs a regulatory opinion.
  • Palm oil. The group requires RSPO or equivalent certification and EUDR due diligence before the palm oil route opens.

10. Open items before investment

  1. Evidence of bulk demand sufficient to support the group's Track A ledger, which is far above the standalone plan.
  2. Group text versus ledger: the text reaches 70% of world exports by Year 5; the ledger reaches it in Year 7.
  3. Group Section 6A loan balance column still uses a $96T base; this document uses $102T, matching Sections 1 and 3.
  4. Signed agreements with the escrow bank, SGS and Proxtera. None is signed as of this document.
  5. Funding for the $1.25M of partner, office, technology and Singapore costs in the earlier draft, which has no line in the group summary.
  6. Treatment of escrow, delivery and other costs passed to customers, and confirmation of the principal or agent position with the accountant.
  7. Income tax base: confirm that the 17% pool tax applies to pre-tax fee income, with a Singapore tax adviser.
  8. Palm oil sourcing policy (RSPO or equivalent, EUDR due diligence) before the palm oil route begins.
  9. Automated customs and trade decisions: a tested human override, a named owner and an audit log, as the group's Addendum A requires.

11. Verdict

Track A now agrees with the group summary on the fee, investor cash, margin, Year 1 loan repayment, Month 12 equity transfer, debt sequence and the escrow bank. The group ledger is the basis for every Track A figure here. The gap is volume: the group's Track A fee income is about 1,800 times the standalone bottom-up Year 10 revenue of $1.73B. Neither document evidences the demand that would close that gap, so the figures should be treated as projections until the open items in Section 10 are closed.
