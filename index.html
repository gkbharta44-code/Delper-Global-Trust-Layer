Delper Global Trust Infrastructure: 15-Year Executive Summary

Revenue streams, capital turnover, rollout, risks, and outlook over a 15-year horizon with 60% of revenue reinvested, based on the source document dated 13 October 2026

1. Bottom Line

Delper is presented as a software-led trade platform that retains part of its revenue to fund its own growth. On the basis used here, with 60% of revenue reinvested and a 15-year horizon, the platform reaches 70% of global GDP in Year 15. Reaching that point requires the reserve to turn over about 13.1 times a year, every year, with no costs, defaults, or delays. The 70% figure measures transaction volume (GMV) against GDP. On a revenue basis the share is far lower.

2. Reinvestment Model

Turns per year (k) = Annual GMV ÷ Reserve deployed
Revenue = GMV × 24.2% take rate
Reserve(t+1) = Reserve(t) + 60% × Revenue(t)
Year 15 target: GMV = 70% × $201.70T = $141.19T

The starting reserve is $3.52M, the document's Year 1 retained figure. Global GDP starts at $126.30T and grows 3.4% a year, reaching $201.70T in Year 15. Each year the full reserve is deployed, turning over the required number of times. Sixty percent of the resulting revenue is added back to the reserve, and the remaining 40% is treated as costs or distributions, which the document does not specify.

5. Revenue Streams

Stream 1: Software take rates

The seller's ERP sends an itemized expense breakout to the Proxtera API gateway through SAP BTP Event Mesh. Delper removes a 5.1% share of the traditional friction cost, leaving a take rate of 24.2% on consumer and high-margin goods and 15.184% on premium commodities. Transactions must be at least USD 300.

Stream 2: Liquidity spread

Delper lends from its reserves on 14 to 90 day facilities at a stated 6.5% p.a. Partner banks take a 0.5% p.a. frontending fee, leaving Delper a 6.0% p.a. net spread secured by bonded cargo.

Stream 3: Sovereign BOOT concessions

Delper funds customs ports, logistics hubs, and data centers under 10-year build-own-operate-transfer contracts and applies the 15.184% rate to port duties and clearing fees. In this summary, BOOT activity begins in Year 6.

7. Breakeven, Feasibility, and Viability

Breakeven. The document projects operational breakeven in Month 4 after launch, and a $20M pilot that returns $3.52M of retained reserves in Year 1. That reserve is smaller than Year 1 revenue of $9.44M, and Year 1 spend of $1.2M would leave about $8.2M. The document does not explain the gap, so the cost assumptions behind the reserve figure should be confirmed.

Feasibility. The design is an integration layer between SAP S/4HANA and the Proxtera API hub, with SGS inspection webhooks and regulatory alignment through Delper Pte. Ltd. with MAS and PDPA. The document does not cite signed agreements for these partners.

Viability. The document argues that traditional friction of 16% to 25.5% can be cut by a fixed 5.1% discount, so Delper captures 94.9% of the friction pool as fee revenue. The discount is a stated input and is not tied to market data.

9. Risks

  • Turnover: The 70% target depends on the platform sustaining about 13.1 turns a year. A missed cycle, default, or operating cost delays the date.
  • Integration dependency: Disruption of SAP BTP Event Mesh or Proxtera endpoints would create transaction backlogs.
  • Adoption limits: Enterprise clients need structured SAP S/4HANA cores, which limits uptake among smaller firms.
  • Regulatory exposure: Antitrust review by the US FTC or European Commission remains a risk. The document's claim of antitrust immunity through BOOT concessions has no cited legal basis.
  • Banking treatment: The Basel III ratios apply to a bank's own capital, so the treatment of reserves inside partner banks needs a regulatory opinion.
  • Settlement and licensing: Netting cannot remove settlement with parties outside the network, and the licences needed for payment, lending, or custody in each market are not addressed.
  • Collateral: The zero-risk treatment of partner banks assumes bonded cargo can always be sold for at least the advanced amount.

10. Verdict and Points to Verify

The document concludes that Delper is structurally sound and recommends immediate global rollout. Its conclusion rests on four claims: defensible positioning through SAP integration, a self-funding capital structure, zero default risk on bonded cargo, and regulatory protection through BOOT concessions. This summary treats each as a claim to verify rather than a conclusion.

  • The Year 1 GMV basis and the bridge from $9.44M of revenue to the $3.52M reserve.
  • The full cost structure between revenue and retained reserves, including the 40% of revenue not reinvested.
  • The turnover capacity, failure rates, and delays across all regions.
  • Signed agreements or public confirmation for each named bank, vendor, and government partner.
  • The legal basis for antitrust immunity and the regulatory treatment of reserves under Basel III.
  • Credit-loss experience on trade lines, including what happens when cargo value falls below the credit amount.

12. Operating Flow: Onboarding to Doorstep Delivery

The operating model connects global customers and vendors through a single integration layer, holds payment in escrow, checks quality at an independent inspection stage, and sources each order through real-time bidding on price and logistics. The stated outcome is a frictionless purchase of the best product at the best price, delivered to the door.

6. Delivery to doorLogistics providers selected through biddingGoods move from the warehouse to the buyer at the winning bid's price and termsDoorstep delivery is not described in the document; logistics costs are not modelled

What the flow changes

  • Price: Competitive bidding can lower the price the buyer pays, but the document's 5.1% saving comes from the Delper fee structure, not from bidding. The two should not be added together without a cost model.
  • Friction: Escrow and inspection add steps and fees. Each one needs a cost line, and none is in the investment plan today.
  • Plan costs: The $4M plan has no line for Tazapay escrow fees or for the bidding and logistics build. Adding them would reduce contingency or require a larger raise.

Points to verify

  • Signed onboarding terms with Proxtera, Tazapay, and SGS, including fees and service levels.
  • The bidding design: who may bid, how the winner is chosen, and how price and logistics are weighed together.
  • Whether Tazapay's escrow fits the internal ledger model and the licensing position in each market.
  • Logistics cost, delivery time, and liability for goods between the warehouse and the buyer.
