Delper Global Trust Infrastructure: Executive Summary

Revenue streams, capital turnover, growth path, risks, and verdict, based on the source document dated 13 October 2026

1. Bottom Line

The document presents Delper as a software-led trade platform that reaches 70% of global GDP in eight years by reinvesting all profit. The 70% result depends on how the share is measured. It is reached only when transaction volume (GMV) is compared with GDP in Year 8. On the revenue basis the document uses for Years 1 to 7, the same inputs reach about 7.9% of GDP in Year 8. Reaching even the GMV target requires the reserve to turn over about 26.4 times a year, every year, with no costs, defaults, or delays.

2. Required Turnover: Highlighted Result

Required turnover to achieve 70% of global GDP
About 26.4 turns per year

Every year, the reserve must be deployed about 26.4 times, or one full cycle every 13.8 days. The document's 14-day cycle gives 365 ÷ 14, or about 26.1 turns, which is slightly below the requirement. The result assumes 100% of revenue is reinvested, a 24.2% take rate, and no costs or delays.

Method

Turns per year (k) = Annual GMV ÷ Cash reserve deployed
Reserve(t+1) = Reserve(t) × (1 + k × 24.2%)
Year 8 target: GMV = 70% × $159.04T = $111.33T

The reserve compounds by the factor (1 + 24.2% × k) each year. Starting from the document's $3.52M reserve, a turnover of 26.4 produces Year 8 GMV of about $111T. The reserve needed at the start of Year 8 is about $4.2T, which equals Year 8 GMV divided by 26.4.

Cash reserve per transaction

Dividing Year 8 GDP by the number of transactions gives the reserve needed to clear each one. The table uses the 70% target of $111.33T and assumes each transaction is funded for one 14-day cycle and then recycled.

3. Revenue Streams

The document describes three revenue streams. All of them are recorded as ledger entries inside the partner bank network.

Stream 1: Software take rates

The seller's ERP sends an itemized expense breakout to the Proxtera API gateway through SAP BTP Event Mesh. Delper removes a 5.1% share of the traditional friction cost, leaving a take rate of 24.2% on consumer and high-margin goods and 15.184% on premium commodities. Transactions must be at least USD 300.

Stream 2: Liquidity spread

Delper lends from its own reserves on 14 to 90 day facilities at a stated 6.5% p.a. Partner banks take a 0.5% p.a. frontending fee, leaving Delper a 6.0% p.a. net spread secured by bonded cargo.

Stream 3: Sovereign BOOT concessions

Delper funds customs ports, logistics hubs, and data centers under 10-year build-own-operate-transfer contracts and applies the 15.184% rate to port duties and clearing fees. The document describes this as starting from Year 5.

6. Breakeven, Feasibility, and Viability

Seed and spend. The seed round is $1.20M: $480K of parent equity and a $720K Singapore venture debt facility. Year 1 spend is $864K of CapEx for SAP BTP middleware, process mapping, and transfer-pricing setup, plus $336K of OpEx for the PwC managed-services agreement and the initial Standard Chartered liquidity container.

Breakeven. The document projects operational breakeven in Month 4 after launch. It also projects that a $20M pilot GMV on the India–ASEAN ONDC corridor yields $3.52M of retained reserves in Year 1, which repays the $720K debt in its first cycle. The Year 1 revenue in the matrix is $9.44M, so the document does not explain how that revenue becomes $3.52M of reserves. Year 1 spend of $1.2M would leave about $8.2M, not $3.52M.

Feasibility. The document describes an integration layer between SAP S/4HANA and the Proxtera API hub, with SGS inspection webhooks, and regulatory alignment with MAS and PDPA through Delper Pte. Ltd. None of these partnerships is supported by a cited contract or public announcement in the document.

Viability. The document argues that traditional supply-chain friction of 16% to 25.5% can be cut by a fixed 5.1% discount, so Delper captures 94.9% of the friction pool as fee revenue. The discount is stated as a fixed input and is not tied to market data.

7. Risks and Strengths

Strengths

  • Closed-loop ledger netting, which the document says removes cash velocity and transfer fees within the network.
  • Self-funded growth from 100% profit reinvestment, with no external financing needed in the document's model.
  • A dynamic pricing model tied to the seller's expense breakout, which gives buyers a verifiable saving on paper.

Risks

  • Integration dependency: Disruption of SAP BTP Event Mesh or Proxtera endpoints would create transaction backlogs.
  • Adoption limits: Enterprise clients need structured SAP S/4HANA cores, which limits uptake among smaller firms.
  • Regulatory exposure: The document's claim of antitrust immunity through BOOT concessions has no cited legal basis. Antitrust review by the US FTC or European Commission remains a risk.
  • Banking treatment: The document says reserves inside partner banks satisfy Basel III capital ratios. Those ratios apply to a bank's own capital, so the claim does not hold as written.
  • Settlement: Netting cannot remove settlement with parties outside the network, and the document does not address the licences needed for payment, lending, or custody in each market.
  • Collateral: The zero-risk treatment of partner banks assumes cargo can always be sold for at least the advanced amount, which the document does not evidence.

8. Inconsistencies in the Source

  • Return on equity: The verdict cites a 70% ROE. The document's own figures, $3.52M profit on $480K of parent equity, give about 733%.
  • Year 1 pilot: The matrix labels Year 1 GMV as $20.00 billion, but its revenue figures reconcile only with a $20.00 million pilot.
  • GDP basis: The 70% GDP claim compares GMV to GDP. The document's revenue-based share is 7.9%.
  • Reserve ceiling: The $29.99T Year 8 reserve exceeds the maximum cumulative revenue the document implies, about $24.5T.
  • Units and shares: The stated share column for Years 1 to 4 is 1,000 times too high.

9. Verdict in the Source and Points to Verify

The document concludes that Delper is structurally sound, highly viable, and high-yielding, and recommends immediate global rollout with market dominance within 96 months. It supports this with four claims: defensible positioning through SAP integration, a self-funding capital structure with a 70% ROE, zero default risk on bonded cargo, and regulatory protection through BOOT concessions.

Before relying on any of these claims, the following should be verified: the Year 1 GMV basis, the reserve bridge from revenue to retained reserves, the turnover and transaction capacity required for the 70% target, the partner and government agreements, the legal basis for antitrust immunity, and the Basel III treatment.

11. Recommendations

  • Present the GDP share on a stated basis, and report the revenue-basis figure alongside any GMV-basis figure.
  • Provide the full cost structure between revenue and net profit, with the assumption behind each line.
  • Reconcile the Year 1 GMV basis and show the bridge from $9.44M of revenue to the $3.52M reserve.
  • Model the required turnover and transaction volume explicitly, including capacity, failure rates, and delays.
  • Provide signed agreements or public confirmation for each named bank, vendor, and government partner.
  • Obtain written legal and regulatory opinions for the antitrust and Basel III claims, or remove them.
